**Figure 1. Study framework and spatial organization of HBV and HPV integration sites.** (*A*) Two possible routes to the same viral–host arrangement: integration followed by local rearrangement, or capture of viral DNA during host break repair. Segments and junctions are schematic. (*B*) The analysis combines integration-site clustering, position within duplicated regions and local structural-variant (SV) class composition. Coordinates are pooled across patients within each viral catalogue and grouped into 100-kb leader clusters. Composition is summarized as (TRA + INV) − (DEL + DUP), using local breakend fractions for translocations, inversions, deletions and duplications, respectively. (*C*) Pooled spacings between adjacent coordinates from the same source sample and locus, compared with independent uniform placement within a 100-kb interval. Only groups with at least two distinct coordinates are included, and the reference preserves each group's distinct-coordinate count (200 simulations per group). Left, cumulative distributions; right, medians and 10th–90th percentiles of spacing (418 HBV and 1,158 HPV observed spacings). Seven zero spacings in the HPV reference are plotted at 1 bp on the logarithmic axis. (*D*) Genome-wide distribution of 304,470 SV breakends, 3,138 retained HBV coordinates and 3,712 retained HPV coordinates in 5-Mb bins. Tracks use separate count scales, with chromosome-terminal bins drawn at their actual widths. The bottom track shows the regions excluded from integration queries, formed by expanding recurrent amplification peaks by 100 kb on each side and mapping the expanded regions to the 50-kb filtering grid. The SV track retains all eligible breakends, including those within excluded regions.
