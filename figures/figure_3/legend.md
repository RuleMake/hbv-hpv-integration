**Figure 3. HPV integration sites are enriched at duplication boundaries.** (*A*) Boundary and interior regions of large tandem duplications (>100 kb; schematic). The first and last 100 kb define the boundary; intervals of 200 kb or less contribute only to boundary counts. (*B*) HPV enrichment and observed coordinate–interval overlap counts in each region. Each coordinate contributes once for every interval containing it. (*C*) Observed boundary and interior shares compared with matched spatial expectations for HPV, HBV and pooled human T-lymphotropic virus 1 (HTLV-1). Coloured segments and printed percentages show boundary shares; grey segments show interior shares, and counts at left give observed overlap totals. Boundary share is B/(B + I), with B and I denoting boundary and interior counts; null shares are derived from mean randomized boundary–interior balances. (*D*) HPV boundary overlaps at baseline (0 kb) and after excluding overlaps within 1, 10 or 50 kb of an endpoint. Exclusions are applied identically to observed and randomized positions. Interior overlaps lie at least 100 kb from either endpoint and are unchanged. (*E*) Relative change in enrichment, calculated as 100 × (fold at the indicated radius / fold at 0 kb − 1). Zero marks unchanged enrichment relative to the 0-kb baseline. Filled and open diamonds in B and E denote boundary and interior overlaps. (*F*) Boundary–interior displacement across the same radii: the observed balance, (B − I)/(B + I), minus its matched-null mean. Displacement equals twice the observed-minus-null boundary-share difference and is displayed in percentage points. Grey rectangles span ± the approximate minimum detectable effect (MDE; 80% power at unadjusted two-sided α = 0.05). Coordinate catalogues are held fixed across radii; D–F use equally spaced radius categories, and connecting lines guide the eye. Whiskers in B and F show 95% spatial reference ranges from 20,000 cluster-preserving randomizations. Fold ranges divide observed counts by reversed null percentiles; displacement ranges subtract reversed, centred null deviations. Further geometry comparisons are reported in Supplemental Material, Section S4 and Supplemental Fig. S9A.
